Before you start

How to review, and what to look for

Do each journey as a founder would: on your phone first, then on a laptop. Answer the assessments with a real business in mind (or the test business below) so the reports have something to say. Use a test email address at the gates; the details go into our own leads table and nowhere else, and we will delete the test rows before launch.

The test business, if you would rather not use a real one.

Northwind Components Ltd. Founder-led engineering business, £6.5m turnover, 42 staff, 14 years old. Wants to sell in three years, has never raised, top three customers are 55% of revenue, management accounts arrive three weeks after month end, no finance director. Answer as its founder would: honest, slightly optimistic.

Five things to judge on every page

  • Brand. Dark header with the red rule, brand red above the fold, charcoal booking buttons, red form buttons, Georgia headings. Anything gold, blue or off-palette is a fault.
  • Words. UK English, founder-first, no jargon, no em dashes, nothing that promises an investor's decision or a match. "Free" only on the assessment, never on the service.
  • Inputs. Every question answerable by tap or keyboard; errors say what to fix; the "Send me my report" opt-in asks for name, work email, company, role and consent and nothing more.
  • Outputs. The full written report renders straight away, with no gate; it should read as a document you would send a client: score, the line to the target, levers, a plan, the next step. Under it, one opt-in offers to email a copy. Print it (Ctrl+P or Share, Print) and check it still looks right.
  • Links. Every link lands where its label says. The Book a call button opens the GoHighLevel calendar, and after an assessment its address carries the assessment code, score and band.
The journeys

Three scripted journeys, one per answer to "What do you want?"

Journey A · "To raise" David

  1. Open the homepage. Read the first screen. Expect: one question, three answers, the red mark. On a phone the doors sit one screen down.
  2. Tap To raise. Expect: the heading changes to "Raising? Start with these." Four doors carry START HERE; the rest stay open. Tap again to clear.
  3. Open the Funding Scorecard door. Answer the 14 questions. Press Calculate. Expect: headline score and band (Raise Ready, Almost Ready or Getting Ready), then the full report immediately: priority gaps, the line to the target, levers, the plan by band, the next step. Below it, "Keep this report".
  4. Try the opt-in empty, then with a bad email, then without consent. Expect: three different plain-English messages. Nothing is stored until it succeeds.
  5. Complete the opt-in with a test email. Expect: "Sending…" for at most six seconds, then "On its way to your address". The email itself arrives only once the send-report function and Resend are configured (see below); until then the lead is stored and the message still confirms.
  6. Press Book your Readiness Call. Expect: the GoHighLevel calendar opens in a new tab. Its address ends with fr_assessment=IR, your score, your band and a source. Do not book unless you want a real slot.
  7. Back on the homepage, open Pitching. Take the eight-statement self-assessment. Expect: no gate; score, band, weakest statements, a route (the Pitching programme, Finance Advisory, or straight to a call) and a written report with Copy summary and Print.
  8. Open Funding from the footer. Paste a paragraph of your own pitch into the language review. Fill the grant builder. Expect: flags read as prompts, not verdicts; the draft assembles in the browser; nothing is stored.

Journey B · "To buy" Marcus

  1. Homepage, tap To buy. Expect: Grow by Acquiring, Commercial Acceleration and Finance Advisory marked START HERE.
  2. Open Grow by Acquiring. Read the seven-phase route. Expect: heads of terms and due diligence written out on first use; the intake link says it opens after a Readiness Call; the funding cards say the platform prepares and never matches.
  3. Take the Ethical Acquisitions Scorecard as a buyer. Expect: the first question asks which best describes you; the result says it is a framework, not an ethical grade; the full report with an automated-suggestion line under the service match, and the opt-in below it.
  4. Open Commercial Acceleration. Read the five workstreams and the commercial model (from Marcus's definition). Take the self-assessment answering "Slightly" to the business case and revenue statements. Expect: the route sends you to Finance Advisory, whatever the commercial score. Answer them "Mostly" and re-run: the route changes.
  5. Take Founder Dependence (linked from Commercial Acceleration and Finance Advisory). Expect: six statements on how far the business depends on you; a route to Commercial Acceleration and Finance Advisory, Finance Advisory first, or a call.
  6. Open Insourcing: Finance Advisory. Check the pricing wording, the four modules and what the scoping review looks at (from Marcus's framework). Take the self-assessment. Expect: entry-level finance operations from £1,000 plus VAT per month, CFO support priced after scoping, no CFO floor shown; the finance route on this page points to the Roadmap.
  7. Open the old address sourcing.html. Expect: it forwards to Finance Advisory.
  8. Open Investors from the nav (Network). Expect: the four-layer boundary in plain words: automated report, adviser recommendation, engagement, offline introduction through the partner. This is the wording awaiting David's sign-off.

Journey C · "To sell" Both

  1. Homepage, tap To sell, open Exit Ready. Expect: the Multiplier Diagnostic, with a paragraph explaining why an exit starts with the whole business and when Transaction Readiness follows.
  2. Answer the 20 questions as Northwind's founder. Expect: six theme scores, top gaps, the service suggestion with its automated-suggestion line, the plan, the offer to go one level deeper, and the opt-in below.
  3. Take the Transaction Readiness Assessment. Expect: 24 metrics with a rubric each; a band from Transaction-Ready to Foundation Tier; the note that answers are self-reported until an adviser verifies them.
  4. Take Personal Exit Readiness (linked from the diagnostic and from Readiness Projects). Expect: eight statements about you rather than the business; a route to Founder Clarity and the Roadmap, Finance Advisory, or the deep assessment; a written report.
  5. Open Readiness Projects. Expect: the 90-day arc with transaction launch conditional on what discovery finds; the three turnover tiers with no gaps; David leading from inside the client company.
  6. Try the Not sure yet? link on the homepage. Expect: the same Multiplier Diagnostic. That is deliberate and the page now says so.
  7. Open Privacy from any footer. Expect: the notice, complete: hosted in the European Union (Ireland), retention 24 months, company details from the live site for David to confirm.
