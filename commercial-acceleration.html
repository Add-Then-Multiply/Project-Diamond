The problem

When a business is sold or funded, someone runs the commercial numbers backwards. They ask whether the customers who generate this revenue will keep paying, pay more, or leave. They look at how much of the revenue sits with the top few customers, whether prices hold or get discounted to close, how many opportunities in the pipeline actually convert, and how much of the selling depends on the founder personally. Each of those is a question a founder-led business usually cannot answer with evidence, and each unanswered question becomes a discount, a deal protection or a reason to walk away. The same questions decide whether an investor or a lender will fund an acquisition, and whether an acquired business can be integrated without losing its customers.

Where it fits

Commercial Acceleration runs through three of the four phases of FACE. In Acquire it works both ways: your own commercial engine is what a target integrates into, and a target's commercial engine is what you diligence before you buy. In Consolidate it is the growth-related sales activity that Insourcing covers alongside the finance function, because a forecast is only as good as the pipeline feeding it. And in Exit it is the commercial due diligence a buyer will run, done by you first. Of the four steps, it sits at step 03, Insourcing, alongside Finance Advisory, once the diagnostic and the roadmap have shown where the engine is missing.
